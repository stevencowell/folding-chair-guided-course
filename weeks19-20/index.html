Week 19: Finish by priority—not panic

Safety, structural integrity and reliable movement come before cosmetic extras. A checklist prevents low-value work from hiding an important fault.

Theory 1

A systematic final inspection follows the chair from structure to surface

Randomly looking for faults usually finds only the most visible issue. A systematic inspection uses the same sequence each time: fixed frame and joints, seat and backrest, pivot hardware, movement path, floor contact and stability, then finish and presentation.

Compare both sides for symmetry. Confirm bolt heads are seated, washers are in the specified positions and locknuts are secure. Move the chair slowly through its complete range and listen as well as look. Scraping, sudden resistance, changing gaps or visible twist provide diagnostic information.

Stability checks occur on a level surface under teacher supervision. The chair should contact the floor as intended and remain geometrically stable in the open position. Do not stand on the project or apply unapproved loads as a test.

1. Safety

Edges and hazards

No sharp edges, loose hardware, exposed defects or dangerous movement zones.

2. Structure

Joints and members

Fixed joints closed, timber sound and components free from damaging splits or excessive distortion.

3. Function

Movement and stability

Controlled folding, correct clearances, acceptable side play and stable open geometry.

4. Surface

Finish and cleanliness

Coating cured, dust-free, consistent and not obstructing hardware or movement.

5. Evidence

Documentation

As-built changes, photos, costs and evaluation match the completed chair.

6. Handover

Workshop condition

Tools returned, waste separated, chemicals secured and faults reported.

Theory 2

Correct the cause, not only the symptom

A symptom can have several causes. A stiff pivot may result from excessive locknut tension, finish build-up, a missing washer, misaligned holes or a twisted member. Wobble may result from uneven floor contact, loose hardware or a racked fixed subassembly. The correct action depends on the cause.

Observed symptomPossible causes to checkLeast invasive first action
Binding at one pointWasher missing, bolt head not seated, holes misaligned, local finish build-up or twisted member.Locate the contact, inspect the hardware stack and test teacher-approved tension adjustment.
Excessive side playLoose locknut, missing/thin washer, oversized hole or compressed timber.Check specified hardware and seating before considering material correction.
Chair rocks openUneven feet, racked frame, surface contamination or twisted member.Use a level surface, identify the high contact and inspect geometry.
Finish scratches during foldingInsufficient clearance, projecting hardware, uncured coating or trapped grit.Stop folding, identify the exact contact and protect the coating while the cause is assessed.

The least invasive correction is the action that restores the requirement while removing or changing the least sound material. Reseating a washer is less invasive than sanding a frame face. Adjusting teacher-approved nut tension is less invasive than enlarging a hole. Major structural faults must be reported; they are not hidden with filler or finish.

!
Do not create a second fault while correcting the first. Every adjustment is followed by the same full inspection and movement test.

Theory 3

Cleaning, maintenance and repair are different responsibilities

Cleaning removes dust, waste and residue. Routine maintenance includes approved care and checks that keep equipment in suitable condition, such as cleaning tables, checking extraction and reporting a blunt or damaged tool. Repair restores faulty equipment and is completed only by authorised people.

Students should not dismantle guards, adjust electrical equipment or attempt specialised machine repair. The correct action is to stop, isolate or tag the equipment as directed and report the fault accurately. A good fault report identifies the machine, the observed condition, when it occurred and what operation was underway without guessing beyond the evidence.

Return tools correctly

Clean approved residue and store clamps so threads and bars are protected.

Protect measuring tools

Wipe clean, prevent damage to edges and return them to the defined location.

PPE has maintenance requirements

Storage, inspection and filter management follow the equipment procedure.

Secure chemical storage

Close lids, clean the exterior as directed and return products to labelled storage.

Clean timber offcuts may be sorted for reuse. Dust, contaminated rags, chemical residues and general waste follow different systems. Leave access paths clear and the bench ready for the next user. Professional responsibility continues after the final photograph.

Week 20: Submit authentic evidence and turn experience into future action

The project is complete when the chair is ready, the portfolio is coherent and the workshop has been handed over safely.

Theory 4

Final submission is a quality-assurance task

Prepare the chair under good light and remove loose dust using the approved method. Confirm the finish is sufficiently cured, the hardware is secure and the chair is identified according to school requirements without damaging the product. Do not make an untested last-minute change simply to improve appearance.

The portfolio should be ordered, readable and consistent with the chair as built. Dimensions use correct units, photographs have meaningful captions, calculations total correctly and the evaluation refers to the original criteria. An as-built record identifies approved differences between the final chair and the earlier drawing or plan.

Submission checkEvidence
Chair open and foldedClear overall photographs and completed movement check.

Submission integrity means the evidence is authentic, accurate and properly acknowledged. Supplied plans and sources are identified where required. Assistance is not hidden, and another person’s image or text is not presented as the student’s own work.

Theory 5

Reflection asks what happened, why and what changes next

Reflection is useful when it links experience to a future action. Begin with a specific event or result. Analyse the cause using evidence from the process. Then state a measurable change that can be applied in another project.

  • 1
    What happened?

    Describe the task, result or fault without exaggeration.

  • 2
    Why did it happen?

    Connect the result to planning, material, measurement, process, time or communication.

  • 3
    What was the effect?

    Explain the consequence for safety, quality, time, cost or function.

  • 4
    What will change?

    State a specific action, checkpoint or habit for next time.

  • 5
    How will it be verified?

    Identify the evidence that will show the improvement worked.

    • “Start earlier” is too vague. “Complete the full hardware dry fit two lessons before finishing and photograph the washer stack at the hold point” is specific and verifiable. Reflection can also identify strengths worth repeating, such as pairing components throughout manufacture or stopping the bandsaw when conditions changed.

    R
    Balanced reflection: identify one practice to keep, one practice to change and one skill to transfer to the next project.

    Theory 6

    Care of the finished folding chair

    The chair should be used only for its intended purpose, stored dry and protected from conditions unsuitable for the timber and selected finish. Moving hardware should be inspected periodically for looseness, damage or unusual resistance. The mechanism should never be forced through a stiff point.

    Cleaning products must be compatible with the finish. Abrasive cleaners or unknown polishes may scratch, soften or contaminate the coating. If the finish wears, the repair system should follow the original product information or another approved compatible process.

    Check the timber around pivot holes, fixed joints and feet for splits, crushing or wear. Standard hardware can often be replaced, but repairs must preserve the original geometry and safe operation. If the chair becomes unstable or damaged, remove it from use until it is assessed.
